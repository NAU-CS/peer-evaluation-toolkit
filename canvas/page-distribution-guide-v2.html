How to use this

  1. Open the section for your team size.
  2. Find the row that best describes what actually happened on your team.
  3. Assign those numbers to real people — the columns are just slots, put the largest number on whoever did the most. Include yourself.
  4. Write one line per person in the survey: nau_email = points

Every row already adds up correctly, so if you copy a row exactly you cannot get the arithmetic wrong. You are not limited to these rows — any whole numbers that total your team size × 100 are fine. These are here so you never have to do the arithmetic yourself.

​

The calculator will not let you finish until your points add up correctly — that is your safety net. There is no separate box asking you to type the total.

If the tool does not load, open it in a new tab. It is a calculator — nothing you type in it is submitted or recorded.

What happens after you submit

One person's opinion will not decide your grade

Every rating you receive is averaged, and that average divided by 100 is your factor. But if fewer than two teammates rate you, your factor is held within 0.10 of 1.00 — because one person's number, on its own, is not evidence. You will see this stated on your result if it applies to you.

If your teammates disagree sharply about you, nothing is posted automatically

When one teammate reports that you did well below a fair share and another reports well above, those two numbers do not average into a measurement — they are a disagreement. In that case no adjustment is posted for you automatically. Dr. Duval reads the evaluations and decides.

Do not try to cancel out a rating you think is unfair

If you believe a teammate is being judged unfairly, rate what you actually observed and explain the situation in the last question — that question goes only to Dr. Duval and never to your teammates. It is exactly the right place for context.

Giving someone an inflated number to offset someone else's low number does not help them. It does three things, all bad: it pushes the team's data past the point where it means anything, it triggers the disagreement check above so nobody on the team gets a result, and it costs you directly — every point you add to one person has to come out of someone, and that someone is usually you.

Your ballot has to balance, or it cannot be counted

You need one line for every teammate including yourself, and the lines must total your team size × 100. If a line is missing or the total is wrong, you still receive the completion credit — but your ratings cannot be applied to anyone's grade, and your teammates lose your voice in their result.

The most common way this happens is leaving out the teammate whose email you are unsure of. Do not guess and do not skip them: the calculator accepts a partial address and will tell you if it cannot match it.
