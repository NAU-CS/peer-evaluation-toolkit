Peer Review Grade Adjustment

​

Most students will receive a 0 here, and that is the expected result. This assignment is worth 0 points. It exists so that credit on the {{ARC_NAME}} draft deliverables can be moved up or down for the small number of students whose teammates report that they did substantially more, or substantially less, than their share. If your team worked evenly, your score here is 0 and nothing about your grade changes.

What this adjusts

Your team receives one shared grade on each group deliverable — everybody on the team gets the same number, exactly as the syllabus says. This line item is the only place where individual contribution changes anything. It applies to the draft half of the {{ARC_NAME}} project:

How the number is calculated

  1. In the peer evaluation, each member of your team distributed N × 100 points across the team, including themselves (N = team size). 100 means “did their fair share.”
  2. We average every rating you received, including your own self-rating. Call that your mean.
  3. Your factor is that mean divided by 100, then limited to the range 0.60 – 1.10. A single harsh rating cannot sink you, and a generous team cannot inflate you without limit.
  4. Your adjustment is (team points earned × factor) − team points earned, rounded to two decimals.

So: factor above 1.00 → positive points. Factor exactly 1.00 → zero. Factor below 1.00 → negative points.

The bottom row is the floor. Even when every teammate independently reports that you did far less than your share, the factor stops at 0.60 — it cannot go lower, no matter how low the ratings go. A unanimous very low rating is also the case most likely to bring you a conversation with {{INSTRUCTOR_NAME}} rather than just a number, because at that point the issue is not a few points.

The fourth column is literally the number that appears in your gradebook for this item: positive when your factor is above 1.00, exactly 0 when it is 1.00, negative when it is below. Because this assignment is worth 0 points, that number is added to (or subtracted from) your project total rather than being scored out of anything.

What you will see

  • A comment on this item showing your mean, how many teammates rated you, your factor, and the arithmetic.
  • Anonymous, aggregated written feedback from your teammates — strengths and areas for improvement, with names removed.
  • You will never be told who gave which number. If your team has two people, be aware your partner can work out your rating from their own result — that is arithmetic, not a policy choice.

If you did not submit a peer evaluation

You still receive your teammates’ ratings, and the adjustment still applies to you. Not submitting removes your voice from the calculation; it does not exempt you from it. You also lose the completion points on the peer evaluation itself.
